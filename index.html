01
Shared governance

One technology, two layers of responsibility

The U.S. framework divides oversight between a national vehicle-safety system and state control of licensing, road use, testing, and deployment. The boundary is deliberate: federal agencies establish a consistent safety baseline, while states determine how automated vehicles enter and operate within their transportation systems.

Federal responsibility

Safety of the vehicle and automated driving system

The U.S. Department of Transportation sets national policy direction, while the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration oversees motor-vehicle safety. Federal tools include performance standards, manufacturer guidance, compliance and defect investigations, exemptions, recalls, and crash-reporting requirements.

  • Vehicle design, construction, and performance
  • Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards compliance
  • Safety defects, recalls, and incident reporting
  • National guidance for automated driving systems
State responsibility

Authorization and operation on public roads

States govern the legal and administrative conditions of road use. Their frameworks may define an automated driving system as the driver, require a human safety operator, authorize driverless testing, set insurance thresholds, or establish application and law-enforcement interaction procedures.

  • Driver licensing and vehicle registration
  • Traffic laws and enforcement
  • Testing and deployment authorization
  • Insurance, liability, and operating procedures

02
Federal evolution

From voluntary guidance to continuing federal oversight

Since 2016, the U.S. Department of Transportation and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration have progressively expanded the federal approach to automated vehicles. The framework balances innovation with public safety through national guidance, regulatory modernization, interagency coordination, and mandatory crash reporting.

  1. 2016
    Federal Automated Vehicles Policy

    A national foundation for automated-vehicle safety

    The first major federal policy adopted SAE automation levels, outlined a voluntary safety assessment, clarified the federal-state division of responsibility, and described existing and potential NHTSA oversight tools.

    Read the 2016 policy ↗
  2. 2017
    Automated Driving Systems 2.0

    Twelve voluntary safety elements

    ADS 2.0 replaced the 2016 policy with a more flexible approach for SAE Levels 3–5. It encouraged developers to document system safety, operating domains, fallback behavior, cybersecurity, validation, data recording, and other core risks.

    Read ADS 2.0 ↗
  3. 2018
    Automated Vehicles 3.0

    Expansion across surface transportation

    AV 3.0 broadened the framework beyond passenger vehicles. It emphasized multimodal safety, reduced policy uncertainty, technology neutrality, stakeholder engagement, voluntary standards, targeted research, and regulatory modernization.

    Read AV 3.0 ↗
  4. 2020
    Automated Vehicles 4.0

    A coordinated federal-government strategy

    AV 4.0 aligned 38 federal components around automated-vehicle development and integration. It connected transportation safety with a broader governance framework for innovation, leadership, collaboration, and public-sector coordination.

    Read AV 4.0 ↗
  5. 2021
    Automated Vehicles Comprehensive Plan

    Collaboration, modernization, and system readiness

    The plan organized federal work around three goals: promote collaboration and transparency, modernize the regulatory environment, and prepare the transportation system through research, demonstrations, and stakeholder partnerships.

    Read the comprehensive plan ↗
  6. 2021–2025
    Standing General Order 2021-01

    Mandatory crash reporting and ongoing monitoring

    NHTSA established mandatory incident reporting for vehicles equipped with automated driving systems and Level 2 driver-assistance systems. Amendments in 2021, 2023, and 2025 maintained an enforceable framework for crash monitoring, defect identification, and safety oversight.

    Read the 2025 amended order ↗
